## Start with the signal, not the spreadsheet

The first thing the buyer should see is why the company is in the pack now. That might be a run of new engineering roles, a team expansion, a new office, or a cluster of commercial hires. If the signal is vague, the pack feels generic before the reader even reaches the next line.

## Make the decision-maker visible early

The pack should not bury the likely contact. Show the most relevant person, their role, and why they are the logical route into the account. That does not need to become a giant research document. It just needs to remove one more layer of uncertainty before the conversation starts.

## Give each prospect a reason to be actioned now

A good pack does not only describe the account. It shows what the recruiter would actually do next. That might be a call angle, a warm-up message, or a note about where the hiring spike points commercially. Without that, the asset becomes information instead of momentum.
